# Carriage: story and framing

A draft. Lines in [brackets] are yours to fill in: only you know them, and the story is stronger for the real
detail.

## The idea in one line

**Carriage turns your iPhone Duo into a typewriter.** Half-fold it like a little laptop: the page stands up on top,
a full typewriter keyboard waits below, and every key sounds like the real machine.

The brand line is **Write with intent.**

## The story

> A while ago I found my parents' old typewriter [where: in the garage? in a closet at their place?]. [What it was:
> make and colour, if you know it.]
>
> I fed a sheet of paper in and started typing, and noticed I was slowing down. Each key took a real press. When
> the bell rang near the margin, I decided where the line ended and pushed the carriage back myself. A typo stayed
> on the page, struck through, so I chose my words before I pressed them. [Optional: what you typed first.]
>
> I was thinking about every sentence, and I was enjoying it.
>
> Our phones are built for creating and consuming more than our brains can take in. I wanted one place on mine
> that asks for less: a page, a line at a time, and the sound of the keys. So I rebuilt that typewriter for the
> iPhone Duo.

Short version, for a bio or a post:

> I found my parents' old typewriter and noticed it made me slow down and write with more intent. Carriage is that
> typewriter, rebuilt for the iPhone Duo.

## How we describe it

Carriage invites you to slow down; it doesn't force you. Describe what it does, not what you can't do.

| Say | Because in Carriage |
| --- | --- |
| **A real press on every key.** | Round keys that go down under your finger, with a real recorded clack and a tap you can feel. |
| **You return the carriage.** | The bell rings near the margin; you finish the thought and push the carriage home yourself, deciding where each line ends. |
| **Mistakes stay on paper.** | X-OUT strikes a typo through with x's, the way it was done on a typewriter. Backspace is in Settings for anyone who wants it. |
| **A room to write in.** | Rain on the window or a fire in the grate, recorded, and a desk lamp that follows the hour. |
| **Quiet.** | Set it up once and iOS silences notifications whenever Carriage is open; the moon clears everything but the page. |
| **Made for iPhone Duo.** | Half-folded, the page stands on the top screen and a full keyboard sits on the bottom. |

**Don't say:** "you can't type fast", "you can't delete", "no taking it back", "that's the point". They aren't true
(backspace and line wrap are settings) and they read as a lecture. Avoid "most advanced phone, most analog app"
contrasts too; they sound clever and say little.

## Duo first

Carriage is made for the iPhone Duo; that's where it's at its best. Lead with the Duo everywhere: the headline, the
first image, the video. Mention other iPhones once, plainly and lower down: "Also runs on any iPhone, with the page
and keys sharing one screen. It's at its best half-folded on a Duo."

## Words we use

Slow down. Intent, on purpose. Analog. The page, the keys, the carriage, the bell. Quiet. A room to write in.

**Avoid:** productivity, efficiency, "write faster", "boost".

## Taglines

1. **Write with intent.** *(the brand line)*
2. A typewriter for your iPhone Duo. *(where people need to know what it is: App Store, Product Hunt)*
3. Slow down. Every letter on purpose.

## The free words

The app gives your first 5,000 words free. "5,000 words" alone sounds like a meter running. Say what it buys:

> **Your first 5,000 words are free. That's a whole short story.**

Then the unlock, as a one-time thing: "Unlock once for unlimited words and every machine. No subscription." The
website shows the launch price and switches to the regular price by itself when the sale ends (October 30).

In the next app update we can carry the same framing into the app's own wording (the counter and the unlock
letter).
