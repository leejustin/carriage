# Carriage: story and framing

## The idea in one line

**An analog typewriter for the most advanced phone you own.**

The iPhone Duo is about as technologically complex as anything you can carry. Half-fold it, and Carriage turns it
into the simplest machine there is: a page on top, keys below, and nothing else.

## The story

> A while ago I found my parents' old typewriter [where: in the garage? in a closet at their place?]. [What it was:
> make and colour, if you know it.] I fed a sheet of paper in and started typing, and something changed.
>
> I couldn't type fast. Every key needed a real press. When the bell rang I had to decide where the line ended
> and push the carriage back by hand. And I couldn't take anything back: every letter went onto the paper for
> good, so a mistake stayed there, struck through with x's. [Optional: what you typed first.]
>
> So I slowed down. I thought before I pressed a key. I wrote less, and meant more of it.
>
> Our phones are built for the opposite: faster, more, everything at once. So I rebuilt that typewriter for the
> iPhone Duo. Half-fold it like a little laptop, and the page stands up while the keys wait below. It sounds like
> the real thing, it feels like the real thing, and it asks the same of you: slow down, and write with intent.

Short version, for a bio or a post:

> I found my parents' old typewriter and realised it made me write with more intent: you can't go fast, and you
> can't take anything back. Carriage is that typewriter, rebuilt for the iPhone Duo.

## What we say, and why it's true

| We say | Because in Carriage |
| --- | --- |
| **You can't type fast. That's the point.** | Every key is a full press with a real recorded clack. The keys stop at the margin until you push the carriage home by hand. |
| **Every letter is on purpose.** | There's no delete. Like a real typewriter, X-OUT strikes a mistake through with x's, and it stays on the page. |
| **The page has an edge.** | The bell rings near the margin, and you decide where the line ends, like real paper. |
| **A room to write in.** | Rain on the window or a fire in the grate, recorded, and a desk lamp that follows the hour. |
| **Your phone goes quiet.** | Set it up once and iOS silences notifications whenever Carriage is open; the moon clears everything but the page and keys. |
| **Made for iPhone Duo.** | Half-folded, the page stands on the top screen and a full keyboard sits on the bottom. |

Backspace and automatic line wrap exist as settings, for people who want them. We mention it in the FAQ, not the
headline.

## Duo first

Carriage is made for the iPhone Duo; that's where it's at its best. Lead with the Duo everywhere: the headline, the
first image, the video. Mention other iPhones once, plainly and lower down: "Also runs on any iPhone, with the page
and keys sharing one screen. It's at its best half-folded on a Duo."

## Words we use

Slow down. Intent, on purpose. Analog. The page, the keys, the carriage, the bell. Quiet. A room to write in.

**Avoid:** productivity, efficiency, "write faster", "boost". Carriage is the opposite of all of those.

## Taglines

1. **Write with intent.** *(recommended as the brand line)*
2. An analog typewriter for your iPhone Duo. *(recommended where people need to know what it is: App Store,
   Product Hunt)*
3. The slowest app on your iPhone Duo. *(playful, good for social posts)*
4. Slow down. Every letter on purpose.

## The free words

The app gives your first 5,000 words free. "5,000 words" alone sounds like a meter running. Say what it buys:

> **Your first 5,000 words are free. That's a whole short story.**

Then the unlock, as a one-time thing: "Unlock once for unlimited words and every machine. No subscription." The
website shows the launch price and switches to the regular price by itself when the sale ends (October 30).
